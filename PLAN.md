Phase 3: Core Statistical ModelingWith your design matrix finalized, you will construct the primary model.Implement Multinomial ordinal logistic regression for ordered responses (Topic 2). Because the response variable (malignancy score from 1 to 5) has a strict rank order but non-uniform spacing, you will fit a Cumulative Logit Model (Proportional Odds Model).The model will estimate a single $\beta$ coefficient for each radiomic feature (representing its multiplicative effect on the log-odds of shifting to a higher severity tier) and $K-1$ distinct intercept thresholds ($\alpha_j$) for the category boundaries.

Phase 4: Latent True-Grade Model via the EM AlgorithmUp to four radiologists rate each nodule and frequently disagree, so the "true" malignancy grade is never observed. Rather than collapsing ratings to a median, treat the true grade $Z_i \in \{1,\dots,5\}$ of nodule $i$ as a latent variable (Dawid–Skene model with covariates).Structural Model: $P(Z_i \le k \mid x_i) = \text{logit}^{-1}(\alpha_k - x_i^\top\beta)$, i.e., the Phase 3 proportional odds model now describes the latent grade.Measurement Model: $P(R_{ij} = r \mid Z_i = k) = \pi_{kr}$, a single $5 \times 5$ confusion matrix shared by all readers (LIDC reader IDs are anonymized and not consistent across patients, so per-reader matrices are not identifiable). Ratings are assumed conditionally independent given $Z_i$.E-step: For each nodule compute $w_{ik} = P(Z_i = k \mid R_i, x_i) \propto P(Z_i = k \mid x_i)\prod_j \pi_{k,R_{ij}}$, computed in log space (log-sum-exp) to avoid underflow.M-step: Update $\hat\pi_{kr} = \sum_{ij} w_{ik}\,\mathbb{1}\{R_{ij}=r\} / \sum_{ij} w_{ik}$ in closed form; update $(\alpha, \beta)$ by a weighted ordinal regression where each nodule appears 5 times (once per grade $k$) with weight $w_{ik}$ (MASS::polr with weights in R).Implementation Details: Initialize from the median/majority rating; run multiple random starts and keep the highest log-likelihood; enforce diagonal dominance of $\pi$ to prevent label switching; stop when the relative change in log-likelihood falls below $10^{-8}$. Use pylidc's scan.cluster_annotations() to group the readers' annotations into physical nodules. If the full $\pi$ is unstable, fall back to a banded $\pi$ or the "one-coin" model.Theory and Validation: Prove monotonicity of the observed-data log-likelihood (Jensen's inequality / KL divergence $\ge 0$) in the appendix and plot it across iterations. Obtain standard errors via Louis' identity or a parametric bootstrap. Run a simulation study with known $(\alpha, \beta, \pi)$ to confirm parameter recovery, and cross-check the covariate-free fit against Crowd-Kit's DawidSkene implementation.Deliverables from this Phase: comparison of $\hat\beta$ (EM) vs. $\hat\beta$ (naive median-rating fit), and a heatmap of $\hat\pi$ quantifying inter-radiologist reliability.References: Dawid & Skene (1979), JRSS-C 28(1), 20–28, doi:10.2307/2346806; arXiv:1812.02736 (crowdsourcing survey, modern notation); McLachlan & Krishnan, The EM Algorithm and Extensions, Ch. 1–4.

Phase 5: Theoretical Diagnostics and Hypothesis TestingTo meet the mathematical rigor expected in a B.Stat analysis, bridge the applied model with likelihood theory.Brant Test: Formally test the Proportional Odds Assumption using a Score test to prove that your $\beta$ coefficients remain constant across all malignancy thresholds (e.g., $Y \le 1$ vs $>1$ and $Y \le 4$ vs $>4$).Likelihood Ratio Tests (LRT): Frame your model evaluation in the context of the Neyman-Pearson framework. Use Wilks' Theorem to compare the log-likelihood of your full model against reduced models, proving the joint significance of your selected radiomic predictors. For the EM model of Phase 4, LRTs must use the observed-data log-likelihood $\log L(\theta; R)$, not the complete-data one.
